# 10 — Electronic Invoicing Research

> Sources: RR 11-2025 digest (S-01), RR 26-2025 digest (S-02), **RMC 98-2026 full text (S-03)**, BIR press release of 2026-09-23 (S-04). All four were read on 2026-10-10. Status: **V-P** for the statements below unless marked otherwise. These are research findings, not legal advice.

## 1. What the sources say

### Who must issue e-invoices (RMC 98-2026 §III; RR 26-2025)
1. Taxpayers engaged in e-commerce or internet transactions classified **Small, Medium or Large**. **Micro taxpayers are exempt.**
2. Taxpayers under the jurisdiction of the **Large Taxpayers Service**.
3. **Large Taxpayers** under RA 11976 (EOPT) and RR 8-2024.
4. **Taxpayers using CAS or CBA with Accounting Records (with electronic invoicing), and other invoicing software.**
5. Others the CIR may require.

The RR 11-2025 digest adds a later wave: exporters, registered business enterprises with incentives, and POS users. That wave applies only *once the BIR system is established*, through a separate RR.

### Deadline
- **On or before 31 December 2026** for covered taxpayers (RMC 98-2026 §IV.1; RR 26-2025). The CIR may extend it further (RR 26-2025).
- Branches: the obligation applies to the head office and **all** branches (§IV.10).

### What counts as an e-invoice (§IV.4)
All three conditions must hold:
- (a) it is generated by a **duly registered, approved or accredited** accounting or invoicing system in a structured electronic format;
- (b) it is transmitted to the buyer digitally (email, online viewing, QR code, app, web platform or similar);
- (c) its data can be electronically extracted, processed and transmitted to the BIR for e-sales reporting.

Invoices made in Word, Excel, Google Docs or Google Sheets are **not** valid e-invoices. A system-generated invoice that is printed, where the system cannot transmit to the buyer and the BIR, is treated as a non-electronic invoice (§IV.5).

### Format
- The EIS prescribes **JSON** for transmitting sales data. Other internal formats are acceptable if they can be converted to the BIR format (§IV.6.a).
- The JSON content is drawn from the minimum invoice information under **Tax Code §113 and RR 7-2024**, plus items such as discounts and withholding taxes (§IV.6.b).

### Corrections (§IV.8)
- An issued e-invoice **must not be deleted, altered or modified**.
- A decrease is made by a **credit note or memo**. An increase is made by a **new e-invoice**.
- Rules on sales adjustments will come in a separate issuance.

### Downtime (§IV.11)
- Issue a BIR-authorized **manual invoice** during downtime. After restoration, replace it with an e-invoice that references the manual invoice number.

### Permits and certification
- A **Permit to Issue (PTI) Electronic Invoice** must be obtained *before* issuing e-invoices. The RDO or LT office evaluates the application within 20 working days, and any change in software or platform requires a new or amended PTI (§IV.12–13). The PTI is **distinct from the CAS Permit to Use or Acknowledgement Certificate** (§II.7).
- **EIS Certification** must be obtained within **6 months** of the PTI, or the PTI may be revoked. Certification involves 5 mandatory tests, or 7 when an API callback is used (§II.6, §IV.15). Portal: `https://eis-cert.bir.gov.ph`.
- A **Permit to Transmit (PTT)** is required only once the CIR directs e-sales-reporting compliance (§IV.15).

### E-sales reporting (Tax Code §237-A)
- This is **separate** from e-invoicing and will apply once the BIR issues implementing rules (§IV.2; S-04). Several secondary sources describe a 3-day transmission window and JWS signing (U); **this is not in the primary text that was read**.

### Electronic Invoicing Service Providers (ESPs)
- Taxpayers may use an in-house system, a commercial solution or an **ESP** (§IV.3). Rules for ESPs will come in a separate issuance; the press release says "within the month" of 2026-09-23. An ESP must be a juridical entity in the Philippines with accountable technical, compliance, data-protection and security officers (§II.5).

### Reclassification (§IV.16)
- A taxpayer moving to a lower classification keeps complying with its approved e-invoicing requirements. A taxpayer moving higher must comply within the period the BIR prescribes, which is at least 6 months.

## 2. What this means for the platform

| # | Implication | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| E-1 | If the platform **issues clients' sales invoices**, it becomes "invoicing software". Every client using it would need a PTI and EIS Certification, and the platform would need to meet the JSON and EIS technical specifications. That is a large regulated product of its own. | **Do not issue client invoices in the MVP.** The platform *records* invoices clients issue elsewhere (decision D-01). |
| E-2 | Item 4 of the coverage list ("taxpayers using CAS or CBA … and other invoicing software") may pull in clients whose **books** are kept on this platform if it is registered as their CAS/CBA. Whether a CAS/CBA *without* invoicing triggers coverage is **ambiguous** in the text read. | **Accountant or tax counsel must interpret** this before deciding whether the platform will be registered as clients' CAS/CBA (decision D-02). |
| E-3 | Covered clients will issue e-invoices from *other* systems. The platform should be able to **import e-invoice data**, which is structured JSON, so recording is accurate. | Phase TBD: an importer for structured invoice data, once the BIR JSON schema has been obtained from the EIS portal. |
| E-4 | Corrections are by credit note or new invoice, never by edit. This matches our immutability design (doc 05). | Model `credit_note` as a business transaction type linked to the original invoice. |
| E-5 | The client's e-invoicing status needs tracking (covered or not, PTI number and date, EIS certification date, deadline). | Add fields to `tax_registrations` or a `client_einvoicing_status` table; show on the compliance calendar. |
| E-6 | The Firm could advise clients on the 31 Dec 2026 deadline. | Out of software scope; a business decision for the Firm. |

## 2a. Update 2026-10-10 — effect on the approved design (D-01 approved; D-02 pending)

The owner confirmed that the platform will **extract and record** clients' existing invoices and **will not issue** invoices in the initial release (D-01 approved).

| Point | Effect | Status |
|---|---|---|
| The 31 Dec 2026 deadline applies to **covered taxpayers issuing invoices** (RMC 98-2026 §IV.1) | The platform issues none, so the deadline does not attach to the platform's own functions. It does attach to **covered clients**, who must issue e-invoices from their own PTI-holding invoicing system by 2026-12-31. | V-P text; per-client coverage **accountant to confirm** |
| Coverage item 4: "CAS or CBA with Accounting Records **(with electronic invoicing)**, and other invoicing software" | If the platform is registered as a client's CBA (D-02) **without** invoicing, the wording suggests this item does not by itself trigger e-invoicing. **This is an interpretation, not a confirmation.** | **Tax counsel** (doc 13 §7 Q4) |
| Under RMO 9-2021 and RMC 98-2026 definitions, a system whose invoices are issued manually or elsewhere is "CBA and Accounting Records", not a full CAS | Shapes how the platform would be described in any registration | V-P definitions; application to confirm |
| E-invoices issued by covered clients must not be altered; corrections are by credit note or new invoice (§IV.8) | Extracted invoice data must be stored **as received** (immutable source record). Accountant corrections happen in the *accounting* layer, never by altering the invoice record. | Design constraint (adopted) |
| From 2027, clients' invoices will increasingly arrive as structured data (BIR JSON) | Plan an e-invoice ingestion path. The **EIS JSON schema is OPEN** (doc 09 D-12). | Roadmap item |
